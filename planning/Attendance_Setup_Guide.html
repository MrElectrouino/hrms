1. The big picture

Three things decide what an employee's day looks like:

QuestionAnswered byWhere you set it
Was the person expected to work today?Holiday calendar, then roster entry, then roster pattern, then weekly off templateAdministration → Attendance setup
How many hours, and from when?The employee's work schedule (or the shift)Attendance setup → Work schedules and Assignments
What happens when they are late, short, or mark late?The attendance policySystem settings → Attendance configuration

Then two things write the day: the employee (or a manager or HR) when they mark, and a nightly job that fills the days nobody marked and works out hours.

The policy only changes values (on or off, minutes, cut-off time). The rules themselves are the same for every country. A country difference is a different policy assigned to that country, not different code.

2. What to set up, in order

  1. Countries, branches, employeesEach employee needs a branch and country of employment, and a date of joining. Policies and holiday calendars find people through these.
  2. Work schedulesAttendance setup → Work schedules. Create at least one, for example "General 9 hours": fixed start and end, required minutes, optional automatic break.
  3. Weekly offAttendance setup → Weekly off. Create a template (for example Saturday and Sunday, or alternate Saturdays).
  4. Holiday calendarsAttendance setup → Holiday calendars. One per company, country or branch, with the holiday dates.
  5. Roster (only if you use shifts)Attendance setup → Roster: repeating shift patterns, or import from Excel.
  6. AssignmentsAttendance setup → Assignments: give employees (many at once) their work schedule, weekly off, roster and holiday calendar, with an effective date.
  7. ShiftsLate and half-day thresholds live on the Shift (start time, "late after" minutes, "half day after" minutes). Make sure each employee who clocks in against a start time has a shift.
  8. Attendance policySystem settings → Attendance configuration → edit the default policy, or add one per country or branch (section 6), then assign it (section 7).
  9. Everyone-settingsSame page: mood and GPS requirements (section 8). Add Ramadan periods if you use a different start time then.
  10. TestMark attendance as an employee on Mark attendance. Next morning check the nightly job filled the previous day (section 11).
Without a weekly off template or roster the system cannot tell a working day from a day off, so the nightly job leaves unmarked days empty instead of guessing Absent. Set this up before relying on Absent.

4. When an employee marks attendance

The employee presses IN and OUT on Mark attendance. Every press is kept as a punch. The day's first IN and last OUT are copied onto the day. Worked time is the sum of each IN to the OUT after it.

One IN and one OUT, and that is it for the day. Once both are recorded, marking is closed for that date — there is no second IN/OUT cycle. The button greys out and shows "You have already marked IN and OUT for today." If a mood question is on, tapping a mood emoji marks IN immediately (no separate confirm step).

5. Late and half day

Compared with the shift start (or the Ramadan start when it applies), at the first IN:

Up to "late minutes" after start: on timeThis is the grace period. Nothing is recorded.
After that, up to "half-day minutes": LateOnly if the policy has Late switched on. If it is off, the day counts as on time.
Beyond "half-day minutes": Half dayOnly if the policy has Half day switched on. If it is off, it falls back to Late (if on) or on time.

Example: shift 09:00, late after 10 minutes, half day after 60 minutes. IN at 09:08 is on time. 09:40 is Late (40 minutes). 10:30 is Half day (90 minutes).

A flexible work schedule has no fixed start. If it has a latest start time, starting after it is Late; with no window nothing is ever late. The late and half-day statuses never overwrite statuses like On leave, Holiday or Absent.

Past dates marked by HR are not judged late unless the policy says Apply late rules to past dates.

7. Which policy applies to whom

On the same page a policy has assignments: one employee, a branch or a country, with from and to dates. The most specific assignment in force wins: employee first, then branch, then country. A person no assignment reaches gets the default policy (Standard).

Example: the default policy for everyone; a UAE policy assigned to country AE with a 10:00 cut-off and Ramadan start times; and one assignment on a single employee who is exempt from late rules. India employees use the default; UAE employees use the UAE policy; that one employee uses their own.

The default policy cannot be deleted, and you can have several policies at once. Policy changes apply to days marked after the change. Days already processed keep their stored policy version.

9. Work schedules, breaks and hours

FieldMeaning
TypeFixed: a start and end time. Flexible: only the required minutes, with an optional window for the start.
Required daily minutesThe time the person must work in a day. Short hours and overtime are measured against it.
Automatic breakMinus break minutes when the person worked at least break after minutes. Example: minus 60 after 300, so a 9-hour stay counts 8.

Hours check (nightly): net time = gross minus automatic break. Short = required minus net, counted only if beyond the grace. Overtime = net minus required minus the "after" minutes. Neither is counted on a holiday or weekly off.

Example: required 480, break 60 after 300, grace 15. Someone in 09:00 and out 17:30 has 510 gross, 450 net: short by 30, beyond the grace, so the day becomes Short hours.

10. Weekly off, holidays, roster

For each day the system decides in this order, and the first one found wins:

1. HolidayThe employee's holiday calendar. A holiday can span a date range.
2. Roster entryA specific shift or off for that employee on that date.
3. Roster patternA repeating shift pattern assigned to the employee.
4. Weekly off templateFixed days (such as Saturday and Sunday), or rules like "2nd and 4th Saturday" or alternate weeks. Assigned with an effective date.

Holiday calendars apply by company, country or branch; the most specific one for the employee is used. If none of 2 to 4 applies, the day is "unknown" and the system will not mark it Absent.

11. The nightly job

Every night at 02:00 the job processes the last two finished days. HR can also run it for a date range (up to 31 days, finished days only) from the attendance processing screen. It is safe to run again.

SituationResult
Day has an IN and an OUTWorked, short and overtime minutes are filled in. Short hours may change the status.
Day has an IN but no OUTLeft alone, and shown in the missing punch report.
No punches, holidayHoliday
No punches, weekly offWeekly off (or On leave if leave covers it)
No punches, working day, approved leaveOn leave
No punches, working day, no leaveAbsent, waiting for review
No roster or weekly off at allLeft empty (nothing guessed)
  • It never overwrites a day a person has approved or corrected; it only refreshes the numbers.
  • It skips inactive employees and dates before joining.
  • Each run is recorded with counts of created, updated and skipped days and any errors.
  • The server must be running at 02:00. If it was off, run it by hand for the missed dates.

12. Corrections and approval

  • Approval. With "Requires approval" on, a manager or HR approves the day (Team attendance). Approving opens a popup where IN and OUT can be corrected before confirming. Approved days are locked for managers.
  • Comp off at approval. The same popup has an "Eligible for comp off" checkbox. Ticking it when approving a day that worked out to a worked holiday or weekly off credits comp off immediately (see the Leave setup guide, section 10) — it is not a nightly job.
  • Regularization. An employee asks to correct a day (forgot to punch, wrong time) with requested IN and OUT times and a reason. Approving applies the corrected times.
  • Manager edits. Only for their own reports, only on unapproved days (if the policy says so), and only to Present, Absent or Not found present.
  • HR edits. Any day, including adding a day with no record, even after the cut-off.
  • Leave approval marks the days On leave. Cancelling it removes them.

14. Not built yet

  • Special periods by shift (for example Ramadan hours as a separate shift list). Today only one Ramadan start time per shift is used.
  • Biometric or device punches. Today marks come from the web app, managers, HR or the Excel import.
  • Geofencing (an allowed area for IN). GPS is collected but not checked against a place.
  • Payroll deductions for late, half day or absent. Attendance only records the day.
